EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This report presents the results of a regression task to predict heart disease output using the Random Forest Regressor. The model achieved an R² score of 0.5977 (59.8% variance explained) with a mean absolute error of 0.2003. Key findings include moderate predictive accuracy, systematic underprediction bias, and opportunities for model improvement through feature engineering.

METHODOLOGY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The analysis used the heart disease dataset with 'output' as the target variable. We evaluated three regression models (Linear Regression, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) using MAE, RMSE, MAPE, and R2 metrics. The Random Forest Regressor was selected for final deployment based on its performance on validation data. Feature engineering and hyperparameter tuning were not performed in this initial analysis.

- RMSE: 0.3130

CONCLUSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Random Forest Regressor achieved moderate predictive accuracy for heart disease output prediction. While the model explains nearly 60% of variance, the 0.20 MAE and systematic underprediction bias indicate room for improvement. The model provides a reasonable baseline but would benefit from feature engineering and hyperparameter optimization to address prediction bias and reduce error.

2. Perform outlier analysis on the 3.1% extreme residual case
3. Test alternative regression algorithms for potential performance gains
